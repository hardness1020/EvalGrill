# Okafor et al. (2025) — Dose-ranging study of NR-7 for sleep quality

*Journal of Nutraceutical Research*, vol. 12(3), pp. 210–224. Lead author: Dr.
Chinelo Okafor, Westbrook College of Pharmacy. Investigator-initiated;
supplement and placebo capsules donated by Halvorsen Bio, which had no role in
design, analysis, or reporting.

## Abstract

**Background.** Trials of NR-7 have used different doses and reached different
conclusions: a large trial at 600 mg reported a modest benefit, while a trial
at 300 mg reported no effect. No study has compared doses directly. We
conducted a four-arm dose-ranging trial to characterize the dose–response
relationship.

**Methods.** 210 adults (age 21–68) with self-reported poor sleep were
randomized to placebo, NR-7 150 mg, 300 mg, or 600 mg nightly (52–53 per arm)
for 6 weeks, double-blind. The primary outcome was change in the Composite
Sleep Quality Index (CSQI, 0–100, higher is better) versus placebo at week 6.
The study was powered for the 600 mg comparison; lower-dose comparisons were
prespecified as secondary.

**Results.** 196 participants (93%) completed the study. Compared with
placebo, mean CSQI change was +0.4 points at 150 mg (p = 0.86), +1.9 points at
300 mg (p = 0.34), and +5.8 points at 600 mg (p = 0.02). A prespecified test
for linear dose–response trend was significant (p = 0.01). Vivid dreams were
reported by 12% of participants at 600 mg, 6% at 300 mg, and 4% at both 150 mg
and placebo. No serious adverse events occurred.

**Conclusions.** NR-7 showed a dose-dependent effect on sleep quality, with a
significant improvement only at 600 mg nightly. These results offer a
plausible reconciliation of previously conflicting trials: the positive
large-trial result was obtained at 600 mg, while the null trial tested 300 mg,
a dose at which we also observed no significant benefit. The 600 mg arm of
this study was modest in size (n = 53), so the point estimate carries wide
uncertainty and should not be treated as definitive on its own. Larger
confirmatory work at 600 mg is warranted, as is caution in extrapolating
benefit to the lower doses most commonly sold.
