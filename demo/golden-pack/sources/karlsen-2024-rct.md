# Karlsen et al. (2024) — Randomized controlled trial of NR-7 for sleep quality

*Nordisk Journal of Sleep Research*, vol. 41(2), pp. 112–129. Lead author: Dr.
Eva Karlsen, Halden Institute for Sleep Research. Funded by the Nordic Sleep
Research Council (no manufacturer involvement).

## Abstract

**Background.** The dietary supplement NR-7 has attracted attention as a sleep
aid on the basis of small uncontrolled studies and animal work. No adequately
powered randomized trial has previously tested it.

**Methods.** We conducted a double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial
in 1,204 adults (age 22–74) reporting subclinical sleep complaints.
Participants were randomized 1:1 to NR-7 600 mg nightly or matched placebo for
8 weeks. The primary outcome was change in the Composite Sleep Quality Index
(CSQI, 0–100 scale, higher scores indicating better sleep), assessed at
baseline and week 8. Secondary outcomes included sleep-onset latency and
self-reported daytime alertness. Analysis was by intention to treat.

**Results.** 1,121 participants (93%) completed the trial; dropout was 7% and
balanced across arms. Mean CSQI improved by 6.2 points in the NR-7 group
versus 2.1 points in the placebo group, an adjusted between-group difference
of 4.1 points (95% CI 1.4–6.8, p = 0.003). Sleep-onset latency shortened by a
mean of 6 minutes versus placebo (p = 0.04). Daytime alertness did not differ
significantly between groups (p = 0.22). Adverse events were mild; the most
common were vivid dreams (10.8% NR-7 vs 3.1% placebo) and transient
gastrointestinal discomfort (5.9% vs 4.2%). No serious adverse events were
attributed to the intervention.

**Conclusions.** In this large randomized trial, 8 weeks of NR-7 at 600 mg
nightly produced a statistically significant but modest improvement in sleep
quality compared with placebo. A 4-point CSQI difference is at the lower bound
of what is generally considered clinically noticeable. NR-7 was well
tolerated. These findings support a real but small effect at the 600 mg dose;
they do not support characterizations of NR-7 as a dramatic or transformative
sleep intervention. Confirmation in populations with clinically diagnosed
insomnia, and at other doses, remains warranted.
