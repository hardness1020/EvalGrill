# Tanaka et al. (2025) — NR-7 at 300 mg for sleep quality: a randomized placebo-controlled trial

*International Journal of Behavioral Sleep Medicine*, vol. 8(1), pp. 44–58.
Lead author: Dr. Rei Tanaka, Kaigan University School of Medicine. Funded by a
national research grant; the authors declare no conflicts of interest.

## Abstract

**Background.** Interest in the supplement NR-7 has grown following
uncontrolled pilot data and one large positive trial at 600 mg. Lower doses
are widely sold and commonly used, but have not been tested in a controlled
setting. We evaluated NR-7 at 300 mg nightly, the most commonly retailed
dose in our region.

**Methods.** Double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial in 412 adults
(age 20–70) with mild-to-moderate self-reported sleep difficulty, randomized
1:1 to NR-7 300 mg nightly or placebo for 12 weeks. The primary outcome was
change in the Composite Sleep Quality Index (CSQI, 0–100, higher is better) at
week 12. Secondary outcomes were sleep-onset latency, nocturnal awakenings,
and treatment satisfaction. The trial was powered (90%) to detect a 4-point
CSQI difference.

**Results.** 389 participants (94%) completed follow-up. Mean CSQI change was
+3.1 points with NR-7 versus +2.3 points with placebo, a between-group
difference of 0.8 points (95% CI −1.1 to 2.7, p = 0.41). No secondary outcome
differed significantly between groups. Adverse events were mild and infrequent
(vivid dreams 6.3% vs 2.9%); no serious adverse events occurred.

**Conclusions.** Twelve weeks of NR-7 at 300 mg nightly did not improve sleep
quality compared with placebo in adults with mild-to-moderate sleep
difficulty. Our findings do not support the use of NR-7 at this dose. We note
that our result contrasts with the positive trial reported at 600 mg; whether
the discrepancy reflects dose, population, or chance remains an open question
that dose-ranging studies are better placed to answer. Claims of benefit
derived from uncontrolled studies should be weighed against the null result of
this adequately powered trial.
