# Internal memo — Meridian Sleep Clinic

**From:** Dr. Ilsa Brandt, Clinical Director
**To:** All clinical staff
**Date:** 3 March 2025
**Re:** Patient experiences with the NR-7 supplement — informal six-month tally

Colleagues,

Since the autumn marketing push we are being asked about NR-7 at nearly every
intake. This memo summarizes what we have actually observed in our own
patient population over the past six months, so that we are at least
consistent in how we discuss it. **This is an informal tally of self-reports,
not a study**: patients self-selected to try the supplement, purchased it
themselves at whatever dose the retailer stocked, and reported back at
routine follow-ups. There was no protocol, no baseline measurement, and no
blinding. Treat it accordingly.

Of the **22 patients** we know to have tried NR-7 for at least three weeks:

- **9** reported a subjective improvement in sleep (descriptions ranged from
  "somewhat easier to fall asleep" to "sleeping through most nights");
- **8** reported no noticeable change;
- **5** discontinued before three weeks — **4** because of unusually vivid or
  disturbing dreams, **1** because of stomach upset.

A few practical observations. First, the vivid-dream complaints are real and
patients should be warned; two patients described the dreams as distressing
enough to outweigh any benefit. Second, most of our patients bought the
300 mg strength, and I note the recent controlled trial at that dose was
negative — several "no change" reports may simply reflect dosing. Third,
nothing here resembles the transformative results in the manufacturer's
marketing; our honest summary to patients should be "some people notice a
modest benefit, some notice nothing, and a few stop because of dream
disturbance."

Until better evidence arrives, my guidance: do not initiate the conversation,
do not discourage motivated patients with realistic expectations, always
mention the vivid-dream caveat, and document dose if a patient chooses to
try it.

— Ilsa
