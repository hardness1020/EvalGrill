# Chen & Aldous (2024) — NR-7 and sleep quality: a systematic review and meta-analysis

*Sleep Science Reviews*, vol. 29(1), pp. 15–33. Authors: Dr. Wei Chen and Dr.
Priya Aldous, Centre for Evidence Synthesis, Marlowe University.

## Abstract

**Background.** NR-7 is widely marketed as a sleep aid. We systematically
reviewed the controlled and uncontrolled human evidence available at the time
of search.

**Methods.** We searched three databases for human studies of oral NR-7
reporting a quantitative sleep-quality outcome, **with a search cutoff of June
2023**. Six studies met inclusion criteria (pooled n = 482): two small
randomized trials, three uncontrolled open-label studies (including Moreau et
al. 2023), and one retrospective cohort. Effects were pooled as standardized
mean differences (SMD) under a random-effects model.

**Results.** The pooled effect favoured NR-7 with an SMD of +0.28 (95% CI 0.05
to 0.51), roughly equivalent to a 9% improvement in typical self-report
scales. Heterogeneity was high (I² = 71%): uncontrolled studies reported
substantially larger effects than randomized ones (uncontrolled pooled SMD
+0.44; randomized pooled SMD +0.11, the latter not statistically significant).
The funnel plot was asymmetric, consistent with possible publication bias
favouring positive small studies. Study quality was rated low or very low for
five of six included studies.

**Conclusions.** The evidence available through mid-2023 is *suggestive but
not conclusive*: the pooled estimate favours NR-7, but it leans heavily on
small uncontrolled studies with inflated effect sizes, and the
randomized-only estimate is compatible with no effect. We caution readers
against quoting the pooled +0.28 figure without this context. Adequately
powered randomized trials are needed; two such trials were registered and
ongoing at the time of writing and are not reflected in this analysis.

## Note for readers

This review predates the large randomized trials published in 2024–2025 and
any subsequent corrections to its included studies. Its search window closed
in June 2023; conclusions should be read as a snapshot of that period, not as
a current synthesis.
