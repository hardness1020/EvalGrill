# García & Lindqvist (2024) — Age-stratified reanalysis of the Karlsen NR-7 trial

*Nordisk Journal of Sleep Research*, vol. 41(4), pp. 355–366. Authors: Dr.
Sofía García and Dr. Anna Lindqvist, Halden Institute for Sleep Research.
Secondary analysis of the Karlsen et al. (2024) trial dataset, conducted with
the original trial team's participation.

## Abstract

**Background.** The Karlsen et al. (2024) randomized trial reported a modest
average benefit of NR-7 600 mg on sleep quality (adjusted difference +4.1 CSQI
points versus placebo). Average effects can conceal meaningful variation
across age groups, which matters for any targeted recommendation. We conducted
a post-hoc age-stratified reanalysis.

**Methods.** Participants were stratified into three prespecified-for-this-
reanalysis age bands: under 40 (n = 381), 40–54 (n = 421), and 55 and over
(n = 402). Within each band we estimated the between-group CSQI difference at
week 8 using the trial's original adjusted model. **This stratification was
not part of the original trial protocol and was not preregistered**;
randomization does not protect comparisons made across subgroups defined
after the fact.

**Results.** The estimated NR-7–placebo difference was +1.8 points (95% CI
−1.2 to 4.8, p = 0.21) in participants under 40; +3.6 points (95% CI 0.2 to
7.0, p = 0.04) in those 40–54; and +9.1 points (95% CI 5.3 to 12.9,
p < 0.001) in those 55 and over. A test of interaction between age band and
treatment was significant (p = 0.01). Adverse-event patterns did not differ
notably by age band.

**Conclusions.** The average benefit reported in the parent trial appears to
be concentrated in older adults, with little evidence of benefit under age
40. These findings are exploratory: subgroup analyses of this kind are prone
to exaggeration and false positives even with an interaction test, and the
parent trial was not powered for them. They generate a hypothesis — that
age modifies the response to NR-7 — that should be tested prospectively.
They do not establish that NR-7 is ineffective in younger adults, nor that
the effect in older adults is as large as the point estimate suggests.
