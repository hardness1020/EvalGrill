# Whitfield et al. (2022) — Oral NR-7 increases sleep-bout duration in mice

*Comparative Sleep Biology*, vol. 6(2), pp. 77–90. Lead author: Dr. Marcus
Whitfield, Ashcombe University Department of Neurobiology.

## Abstract

**Background.** NR-7 is a small-molecule supplement with structural similarity
to endogenous purine metabolites. In vitro work suggests it modulates
adenosine A1 receptor signalling, a pathway central to sleep pressure. No in
vivo sleep data have been reported.

**Methods.** Forty adult C57BL/6 mice were randomized to standard chow or chow
supplemented with NR-7 (dose scaled to approximate a human 600 mg equivalent)
for 14 days. Sleep architecture was measured by EEG/EMG telemetry over the
final 72 hours. Primary outcomes were mean sleep-bout duration and total NREM
sleep time during the light (rest) phase.

**Results.** NR-7-fed mice showed a 38% increase in mean sleep-bout duration
(p < 0.001) and a 22% increase in total NREM sleep time (p = 0.002) relative
to controls, with no significant change in REM sleep time. Sleep-onset
latency after light onset was reduced by 27% (p = 0.01). Ex vivo receptor
binding assays in a satellite cohort were consistent with partial A1 agonist
activity. Body weight, food intake, and open-field locomotor activity did not
differ between groups, arguing against gross sedation.

**Conclusions.** Dietary NR-7 substantially consolidated sleep in healthy
mice, consistent with the proposed adenosinergic mechanism. We caution
strongly against direct translation to humans: murine sleep architecture
differs fundamentally from human sleep, allometric dose scaling for this
compound class is uncertain, and effects in healthy animals may not predict
effects in individuals with sleep complaints. These results motivate — but
cannot substitute for — controlled human trials. In our view the appropriate
public conclusion from this study is mechanistic plausibility, not efficacy.
