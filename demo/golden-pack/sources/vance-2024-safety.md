# Vance & Osei (2024) — Safety profile of oral NR-7: a pooled review of adverse-event data

*Drug and Supplement Safety Bulletin*, vol. 22(6), pp. 401–412. Authors: Dr.
Harriet Vance and Dr. Kwame Osei, Independent Pharmacovigilance Group.

## Scope

This review pools adverse-event (AE) data for oral NR-7 from published human
studies, unpublished trial safety appendices shared by investigators, and two
national supplement-surveillance registries, covering an estimated **1,900
person-exposures** at doses from 150 mg to 600 mg nightly and durations up to
12 weeks. **This review assesses safety only. It draws no conclusions about
whether NR-7 improves sleep, and its data cannot be used for that purpose.**

## Findings

NR-7 was generally well tolerated across all identified sources. The most
consistently reported adverse events were:

- **Vivid or unusual dreams — approximately 11% of exposed individuals**
  (range across sources 6–12%), versus roughly 3% in comparator/placebo
  groups where available. Frequency appeared dose-related, with the highest
  rates at 600 mg. Most reports were mild; a minority described the dreams as
  distressing, and this was the most common single reason for
  discontinuation.
- **Transient gastrointestinal discomfort — approximately 6%** (nausea,
  stomach upset), typically resolving within the first two weeks without
  discontinuation.
- Infrequent reports (<2% each) of morning grogginess and headache, at rates
  similar to comparator groups.

**No serious adverse events attributable to NR-7 were identified** in any
reviewed source. No signal emerged for dependence, withdrawal effects, or
next-day cognitive impairment, though formal driving/psychomotor studies have
not been conducted. Data in pregnancy, in adolescents, and alongside sedating
medication are absent; use in those contexts cannot be assessed.

## Conclusions

Across roughly 1,900 documented exposures, NR-7's short-term safety profile
is benign, with dose-related vivid dreams the principal tolerability issue
and mild gastrointestinal symptoms a secondary one. Longer-term safety beyond
12 weeks is uncharacterized. We recommend that any counselling about NR-7
include the vivid-dream caveat, and we repeat: efficacy is outside the scope
of this review and is not addressed by these data.
